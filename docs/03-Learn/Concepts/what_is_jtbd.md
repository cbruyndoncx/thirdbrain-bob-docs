# What is Jobs To Be Done (JTBD)

## The Core Idea

People don't buy products — they **hire** them to get a job done.

A customer doesn't want a drill. They want a hole in the wall. They don't even want the hole — they want the shelf hung, the picture displayed, the room looking the way they imagined. The drill is hired to make progress toward that outcome.

**Jobs To Be Done** is a framework for understanding *why* people make the choices they make, by focusing on the progress they're trying to achieve in a specific circumstance.

---

## The Job Story Format

Traditional user stories center on personas: *"As a [role], I want [feature], so that [benefit]."*

Job stories center on situations:

> **When** [situation/trigger], **I want to** [motivation], **so I can** [desired outcome].

### Examples

| Job Story | What It Reveals |
|-----------|-----------------|
| When I have a meeting in 5 minutes, I want to scan my notes quickly, so I can show up prepared without stress. | Speed matters more than comprehensiveness. |
| When a prospect asks "why you?", I want a comparison I can send immediately, so I can keep the deal momentum going. | The job is confidence, not just information. |
| When I finish a client project, I want to capture what worked, so I can replicate success next time. | The real job is *future performance*, not documentation. |

The power is in the **"When"** — it anchors the job in a real moment, not an abstract persona.

## Three Dimensions of Every Job

Every job has three layers. Most teams only design for the first one.

### 1. Functional

The practical task. *Get the report done. Send the invoice. Publish the post.*

### 2. Emotional (Personal)

How the person wants to **feel**. *Confident I didn't miss anything. Proud of what I shipped. In control of my workload.*

### 3. Social

How the person wants to be **perceived**. *Seen as competent by the client. Recognized as thorough by my team. Trusted with bigger projects.*

When a solution only addresses the functional job, it feels utilitarian. When it addresses all three, it feels indispensable.

## The Four Forces

When someone considers switching from their current approach to a new one, four forces are at play:

```
          PUSH                          PULL
  ┌─────────────────┐          ┌─────────────────┐
  │ Frustration with │          │ Attraction of    │
  │ current solution │ ───────▶ │ new solution     │
  └─────────────────┘          └─────────────────┘
          ▲                            │
          │         SWITCHING          │
          │                            ▼
  ┌─────────────────┐          ┌─────────────────┐
  │ Habit of the     │          │ Anxiety about    │
  │ current way      │ ◀─────── │ new solution     │
  └─────────────────┘          └─────────────────┘
```

**Forces driving change:**
- **Push** — Pain with the current approach (*"I keep losing track of what the agent needs from me"*)
- **Pull** — Appeal of the new way (*"Imagine if the agent just told me exactly what it needs and waited"*)

**Forces resisting change:**
- **Anxiety** — Fear of the unknown (*"What if this new system is too rigid?"*)
- **Habit** — Comfort with the status quo (*"My current process works fine... mostly"*)

For someone to switch, Push + Pull must outweigh Anxiety + Habit.

## Common Mistakes

### Confusing jobs with tasks
A task is *what you do*. A job is *why you do it*. "Write a blog post" is a task. "Establish authority so prospects trust me enough to buy" is the job.

### Confusing jobs with features
"Add a kanban board" is a feature. "See at a glance what's stuck and what's moving" is the job the kanban is hired for.

### Skipping the situation
"I want to track my time" is vague. "When a client questions my invoice, I want to show exactly what I did, so I can justify the fee without awkwardness" — that's actionable.

## Why JTBD Matters for This Vault

This vault contains skills — tools that know *how* to do things. But knowing how to write copy, run an audit, or generate a report doesn't answer:

- Which skills to combine for a specific outcome?
- In what order?
- Where does the human need to weigh in?
- What context does the agent need upfront?
